- **Weirs, locks and pools on the map (MAP-4).** The map now shows where the design's pools and weirs are. The engine makes a typed list on demand from the water level along the design line (`owd_format::design::structures`, `Session::map_structures`): a pool is a stretch where the level is flat, and a weir site is a place where the level steps, with its chainage, its position, the line's bearing, the pool level upstream and downstream, and the lift. It reads the optimise stage's pools when it has run and a pool table (`owd import --water-pools`) otherwise; a constant level or a straight fall has no step, so it gives an empty list. The Layers list has three new ticks. **Weirs and locks** (on at first; greyed and "none" with no pools) draws at each site a weir as a bar across the river, clipped to the river polygon's width there (100 m with no polygon), and a lock chamber on the design line, with a numbered mark at any zoom and a label from zoom 9 in ("Weir 7 · km 110.3 · pool 49.0 m · lift 7.0 m": the pool level is the one the weir holds, upstream). A click opens a card: chainage, upstream pool level, downstream level, lift, lock chamber size, weir length and the land its pool floods. **Pool levels** (on at first) lays a pale band along each pool and a label at mid-reach ("Pool 8 · 49.0 m"). In the 3D view each weir and lock stands up as blocks. This is a **placeholder model, not a design**, and every name of it says so: chamber length 1.2 x vessel length, chamber width vessel beam + 2 m, walls, gates and weir 3 m thick, top of walls 1.5 m above the upstream pool level, weir crest at the upstream pool level, chamber water at the lower pool level. The map measures a block's height from the terrain at the block's middle, so the page works each height out from the drawn terrain to put the top at its level. **Pool water** (off at first; greyed with no pools or no DEM) shows the ground each pool floods as translucent water, darker where deeper, with a depth key in the legend. A ground cell is flooded when the nearest point of the design line is in the pool, its DEM level is below the pool's level, and it is joined to the design line or the river polygon through other flooded cells, so a hollow far from the river stays dry. At a weir the two levels meet on the line across the river through the site, and the upper pool's water does not pass it. The engine works this out once per DEM and pool set on a flood mask of at most 4,000,000 cells (`owd_format::design::pool_water`), then makes each map tile on demand in the browser, off the page's thread, under schemes of its own (`owd-pool://`, `owd-pool-dem://`); tiles read the DEM at its full detail. A click on the water reads "water level 49.0 m, ground 44.5 m, depth 4.5 m, pool 8". In 3D the map stands on a second terrain whose height is the pool's level where there is water, so each pool is a flat surface. A **Land** button beside the tick opens a table of each pool's flooded area and the part outside the river polygon, in hectares. Nothing is sent to any server. The ticks are remembered on this machine (browser storage), never in the design file: the design file and every export are unchanged. The desktop app has the weirs and pool levels; its Pool water tick is off and says "web app only for now".
