- **A pool whose search cannot close on its end pose no longer leaves a stepped join, and a loop in the pool lattice's line is reported.** When a pool's search found no closing path to its end pose, it fell back to a state within `goal_pos_tol_m` and one heading bin, and that line was spliced in as it was: the join stepped sideways, read far under R_min, and with a larger gap and a heading miss the joined line could cross itself. Now such a pool is searched again with its search end moved along the baseline, in steps of a quarter of R_min (`BOUNDARY_STEP_FRACTION`), up to four steps (`BOUNDARY_STEPS`, so up to one R_min) downstream first, then upstream, each moved end staying at least one step inside the stretch it moves into. The first end that closes is kept: the pool record carries `end_moved_from_m`, and when the end moved downstream the next pool's search starts there (`start_moved_from_m`); moved upstream, the baseline keeps the stretch between. Only the search boundary moves: water levels and weirs do not change, and a sanity warning names the move and the stretch to check. When no end closes, the fallback line is spliced only if its join holds R_min and the joined line does not cross itself there; otherwise the pool keeps the baseline, with the reason and a sanity warning. The result now carries `self_crossing_m`, the chainage where the joined line first crosses itself (null when it does not), and the line's sanity check already warns with that chainage. With `close_goal` false (the older search) fallback lines are spliced as before and their joins are reported. On a synthetic narrow valley round a 260 m bend (R_min 340 m) with the pool boundary at mid-bend, the first pool now closes on an end moved 255 m downstream, and the join reads R_min instead of about 116 m (issue #565).
