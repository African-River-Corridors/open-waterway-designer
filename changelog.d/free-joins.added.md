- **The pool search no longer has to pin its joins to the baseline: `seams_on_straights`, `free_seams`, `min_straight_m` (OPT-3).** The lattice searches each pool, and each 6 km piece of a long pool, between two end poses. Both were pinned to the baseline's exact point and heading, so a join on a bend forced a curve of R_min beside it and a join where the baseline is off the river forced the line off the river. Three optimise settings change that; all are off by default, are not written into a design at their defaults, and leave every existing result byte for byte the same. `seams_on_straights` (`owd optimise --seams-on-straights`, window `--seam-window`, 1,500 m) puts each seam of a long pool where the baseline is straightest and inside the river polygon. `free_seams` (`--free-seams`) lets the line pass a seam through a gate (the river's width on the cross-line, or `--seam-offset`; heading within `--seam-heading-tol`, 30 degrees) and starts the next piece from the pose the line ended on; at a weir site between two pools the line runs straight for `--weir-straight` (two vessel lengths by default) through the gate, half each side. A seam or weir site that cannot be freed is pinned as before and says why by name, in the details and in a warning. `min_straight_m` (`--min-straight`) holds a straight of at least that length between a left curve and a right curve, as a rule of the search's state. The lattice's details list every seam (`seams`) and weir site (`weirs`). The web app's Optimise row has a control for each. On a synthetic river whose baseline runs on the bank 70 m beside the river, free seams cut the search cost from 540,799 to 151,285.
