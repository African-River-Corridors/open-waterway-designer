- **The rolling circle no longer leaves a corner where one bend turns into the next.** Where the old line turned the other way past a circle's touch, no straight could leave the circle and meet it, so the circle fell back to the crossing exit and left a corner between the two bends (17 degrees on an 8 m station on a synthetic chain of 120 m bends, against 1.9 for a circle of R_min 240 m). Such a circle now takes a reverse exit: its arc, the inner common tangent, and an arc of R_min turning the other way that meets the old line tangent at a vertex (a Dubins RSL or LSR join). The crossing exit is kept only where no reverse exit fits. A circle with a reverse exit records `tangent_exit: true`.
