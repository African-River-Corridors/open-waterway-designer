- **A long pool is searched in pieces, and a pool whose search hits its limit says so.** The pool lattice searched each pool in one A* run, capped at `max_expand` (4,000,000 expansions). On a long open-river pool (tens of km at one water level) the search hit the cap, returned no line, and the pool silently kept the baseline. A pool longer than `LatticeConfig::max_pool_piece_m` (default 6,000 m, `DEFAULT_MAX_POOL_PIECE_M`) is now searched as consecutive pieces at the same water level. Each piece starts and ends on the baseline's pose at its boundaries, so the pieces meet tangentially, and each piece join is read for R_min and reported like a pool join. The rule for the boundaries (`pool_pieces`): `ceil(length / max_pool_piece_m)` pieces as even as they can be, each inner boundary moved up to an eighth of a piece (so no piece is longer than 1.25 x the setting) to where the baseline turns least over R_min either side, because a boundary on a tight bend pins the line to a pose that can break R_min. Each pool record in the lattice option's details now carries `piece` (index, count and the whole pool's ends, or null), `expansions` and `hit_cap`. A pool or piece that hits the cap before it finds a line keeps the baseline with the reason "the search hit its expansion limit" and a sanity warning names it ("the pool from A m to B m was not searched: the search hit its limit of N expansions, so it keeps the baseline"). Why 6 km: on a synthetic sine river with uneven dry ground (amplitude 250 m, wavelength 1,600 m, river 30 m wide, R_min 340 m, the optimise stage's default pitch 12 m, cell 2 m and rake), one search took 0.13 M expansions for a 3.0 km pool, 0.92 M for 6.1 km, 1.83 M for 9.1 km and 2.62 M for 12.1 km (about 2.7 M states and 1.9 GB of memory at its peak): the work grows faster than the length, a 6 km piece uses under a quarter of the default cap and about 0.7 GB, and a pool of 6 to 7 km of a real, uneven river converges in seconds where tens of km hit the cap (issue #559).
