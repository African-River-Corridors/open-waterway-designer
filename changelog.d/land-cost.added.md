- **The pool search can pay for land: `land_weight`, a cost per m2 of channel outside the river polygon (OPT-1).** Ground that a pool drowns deeper than the channel bed costs nothing to cut, river or floodplain alike, and the search had no cost for land take. So a design line could pass its checks while it ran over drowned floodplain, hundreds of metres from the river. The new optimise setting `land_weight` charges every m2 of the channel's footprint that lies outside the river polygon, whether or not any cut is needed there. The footprint is the channel's plan width at the water surface: the bottom width (the arc's own widened width on an arc) plus the side slopes up through the design depth. The unit is m3 of cut per m2, so a weight equal to the channel depth prices land like a full-depth cut. Every lattice edge, closing path and the baseline the line is compared with pay it, so the keep-the-baseline rule compares like with like; the `c_min` heuristic prices it the way it prices cut. Set it with `owd optimise --land-weight X`, or in the app's Optimise row ("Cost per m² of land"). It is stored in the settings and is part of the optimise hash. The lattice's details show the weight, the footprint's width (`land_footprint_width_m`) and, for each pool, `land_area_m2`, `land_cost` and `baseline_land_area_m2`. The default, 0, is off: the setting is then not written, and every design gives the same line and numbers, byte for byte (a test pins the optimise results of the `optimise_pools` example to the bytes of the branch before this change). The volumes stage is not changed: it reports the m3 it measures, never the land cost. The weir cascade does not read the setting. On a synthetic drowned plain (a 120 m river round a sine meander whose bends, at 292 m, are tighter than R_min 340 m; a 61 m footprint; length weight 1):

  | land_weight | share of the line outside the river | furthest from the river's centreline (m) | land taken (m2) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 0.64 | 301 | not measured |
  | 0.005 | 0.32 | 309 | 82,207 |
  | 0.02 | 0.00 | 39 | 2,375 |
  | 3.5 (the depth) | 0.00 | 29 | 80 |
