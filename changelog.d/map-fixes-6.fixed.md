- **The Cut extent layer no longer draws cut where the volumes found none.** A point beside a stretch with no cut (or behind the start of the design line) did not see that stretch, so it took the last station with a cut as its nearest. That station's cut was then drawn in a wedge out to its full reach, hundreds of metres. On high ground the wedge read as the deepest dredge class: a flat navy wash. The stretches with no cut now count as the nearest part of the line, nothing is drawn past an end of the line, and a side is cut between two stations only when both have a cut on it. A test checks that the area drawn matches the plan area of the stations' own cut (within 1 %), and that nothing is drawn farther out than the widest daylight offset.
- **No more dashed daylight lines across the channel.** Where a cut was on one bank only, the other bank's daylight line was drawn on the design line, so it jumped across the channel wherever that side's cut started or stopped. Each bank's line now runs only along the stations where that bank has a cut that meets the ground at or outside the water's edge. It ends at the last such station and leaves a gap where there is no cut. Close in, the cut-depth picture is made three zoom levels past the DEM's own, so its edge follows the daylight line and not a staircase of DEM-sized pixels.
